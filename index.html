Conclusion

Only two colleges land in the top tier (20 or more matriculants) at every one of the five schools: Georgetown University and Cornell University. That is a small, tight overlap given how differently these five schools are sized and how differently each window of years was measured — it says something real about the shared appeal of those two schools across the whole boarding-school landscape, not just one or two of these campuses.

Harvard, Yale, and Brown are in the top tier at four of the five schools, missing only at Hotchkiss — but that's mostly a function of Hotchkiss's much smaller graduating class (≈160/year vs. 225–300 elsewhere) rather than weaker interest; Harvard's 18 Hotchkiss matriculants would clear 20 at most other schools' pace if scaled up proportionally.

Choate Rosemary Hall's numbers are the most concentrated of any school here in absolute terms: University of Chicago (63), New York University (54), and Yale (42) are unusually high raw counts, but Choate also has the largest graduating pool (≈1,250 over five years), so as a share of the class they land around 3–5%, similar to the concentration Hotchkiss shows with Georgetown (~5%) despite Hotchkiss being roughly half Choate's size. Lawrenceville shows its own strong concentration toward Cornell (38) and University of Pennsylvania (36).

Phillips Academy remains the most evenly spread of the five, sending 5 or more students to over 50 different colleges rather than concentrating heavily on a handful. Across all five schools, the picture is consistent: a small set of highly selective universities (Georgetown, Cornell, Harvard, Yale, Brown, Columbia, Penn, NYU, University of Chicago) absorb a disproportionate share of graduates, while a long tail of 60–90 other colleges each take a handful of students per school. None of this reflects admissions difficulty or outcomes — only where each school's graduates chose to enroll.
